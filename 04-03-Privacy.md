**Privacy Arguments Against ALPRs**

* As evidenced in the [coverage about ALPRs being used for immigration enforcement](?tab=t.warkykfk5o0y) (often in direct defiance of local policy created by communities to keep immigrant neighbors safe), no guardrails have proven sufficient to keep data out of the hands of federal agencies or neighboring police. Guardrails approaches are outdated, especially in this environment.   
* ALPRs are not just harvesting our license plate numbers \- they are collecting large troves of data and information about us without our informed consent.   
  * [Flock feeds data fusion tools](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/dangers-unregulated-ai-policing\)) which can build up profiles using public records and their social media usage. This tool also utilizes AI to flag certain drivers as suspicious based on driving patterns. These companies are feeding into these tools that are tracking everything about us, without our consent.  
  * This [Wired article](https://archive.is/NhXsR) about data insecurities shows 150 Motorola ALPRs had exposed live footage to anyone on the internet and were leaking data, and it also mentions that ALPRs capture information beyond license plates including lawn signs and t-shirts.   
* ALPRs are invasive tech that gives people we don’t know access to live streams of sensitive areas.   
  * In Dunwoody, GA, ALPR sales staff [accessed footage of children in a gymnasium](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/flock-safety-employees-watched-kids-181539475.html) without their parents’ permission for a sales pitch to police.   
* Flock ALPRs [use AI to determine whether individual behavior patterns are “suspicious”](https://www.aclu.org/news/national-security/surveillance-company-flock-now-using-ai-to-report-us-to-police-if-it-thinks-our-movement-patterns-are-suspicious), meaning you could be targeted based on an algorithm rather than actually engaging in illegal behavior.  
* Companies are now bundling ALPR data with known Bluetooth signals that a particular vehicle emits, which lets them do things like [link your car to your phone, airbuds, smartwatch](https://www.404media.co/this-company-will-add-phone-airpod-and-smartwatch-trackers-to-license-plate-readers/), etc.   
* During a Flock [webinar](https://flocksafety.wistia.com/medias/cvkcua786f), company reps discussed how camera “hot spots” can be used to identify patrol routes for police (potentially exacerbating the overpolicing of communities) and acknowledged how their camera networks can be used to search for individuals (like a person wearing a jacket with the state of Texas on it).   
* Flock leaked some of the license plates they were searching and why
they were being investigated on DuckDuckGo. [https://www.404media.co/flock-leaked-cops-license-plate-searches-via-duckduckgo-bing/](https://www.404media.co/flock-leaked-cops-license-plate-searches-via-duckduckgo-bing/)
