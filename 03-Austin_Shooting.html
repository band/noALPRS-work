Austin Shooting - A Review of the Facts

We believe the Austinites impacted by the violence that unfolded the weekend of May 16 should have time to process in peace - but unfortunately, peace is not what is being prioritized by our Mayor, police department, or the Flock executives with whom they are transparently coordinating a messaging campaign in support of mass surveillance. Because of that, we feel compelled to respond to the disinformation and claims they have made below.

A review of the facts:

  • The teenagers who carried out the shooting allegedly stole their firearm from Central Texas Gun Works, a theft perpetrated by requesting to see a gun and running out of the store without paying for it. According to Texas law, guns may not be legally sold to underage persons, but the gun shop failed to ID the teenagers prior to putting the deadly weapon in their hands.
    • One of the suspects had a warrant out for his arrest, which APD failed to execute, prior to the shooting.
  • Michael Cargill, the owner of Central Texas Gun Works, has been at the center of repeated incidents of violence and has personally advocated to block or reverse reasonable gun reforms that would save lives.
  • On Sunday afternoon following the shooting’s conclusion (May 17), APD and Mayor Kirk Watson held a press conference in which they asserted APD could have apprehended the shooters sooner had the city not canceled our contract with Flock, Austin’s previous ALPR vendor. They claimed ALPRs “would have helped” and signaled an intention to bring ALPRs back to Austin.
    • However, ALPRs are in fact currently up all over the city. After Austin’s ALPR pilot program ran from 2024-2025, City Council allowed the contract with Flock to conclude without renewal, leading to the end of our program. By the end of 2025, all Flock cameras had been removed from city property. In early 2025, however, the Department of Public Safety (DPS) put Flock ALPR cameras on state-owned right-of-ways throughout Austin in defiance of local political will. The talking points used by APD and the Mayor are based on a lie.
  • Flock executives including their Founder & CEO, Chief Communications Officer, and Chief Strategy Officer made public statements within days of the shooting’s conclusion, shaming City Council for enacting the will of constituents and concluding our ALPR program, blaming Austinites for the violence that befell our community, and taking the opportunity to manipulatively hock their surveillance technology products.
  • Just one day after Austin’s shooting, another shooting was perpetrated in San Diego, with shooters killing three people before committing suicide. In San Diego, as in Austin, ALPRs are up all over the city. In San Diego, ALPRs neither prevented nor stopped the shooters. Flock has been strangely silent in regard to the San Diego shooting.

If ALPRs are able to stop violence as clearly and consistently as the vendors claim, why did the ALPRs in Austin and San Diego both fail to apprehend these shooters? Austinites should not be asked to endure random acts of gun violence, then be punished again for these horrific acts by having our civil liberties curtailed by mass surveillance that does not solve the problem.
