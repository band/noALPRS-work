Police Abuse Arguments Against ALPRs

  • Police have repeatedly abused their access to ALPRs to stalk their partners or ex-partners.
    • Police Family Violence Fact Sheet - details research from the early 1990s investigating the prevalence of domestic violence in police families.
    • In 2024, the former police chief of Sedgwick, Kansas abused ALPRs to stalk his ex-girlfriend, making 228 searches of her vehicles over four months and following her and her new partner in his police cruiser.
    • Another 2024 case out of North Charleston, South Carolina involved a police lieutenant who was merely demoted after using ALPRs to stalk his wife, who he suspected of having an affair.
    • In 2025, a story broke detailing a Florida police officer's frightening pattern of stalking, control, and abuse of his then-girlfriend using ALPRs, executing over a hundred illegal searches.
    • In 2026, a Milwaukee police officer was investigated for abusing ALPR access to stalk his ex-partner. The story references several other instances of police abuse.
  • Police have repeatedly abused their access to ALPRs to target individuals over personal vendettas that have nothing to do with law enforcement.
    • A story broke in 2025 about a police chief in the Atlanta suburbs who abused ALPRs to stalk and harass multiple people who were not being investigated in relation to crimes.
  • Police used ALPRs to search for Romani people, using racist terms to target people in this ethnic group in violation of stated policies asserting ethnicity or race should play no role in the investigation or prosecution of crimes.
  • Police abuse of ALPRs is expensive, costing municipalities millions of dollars in legal settlements.
